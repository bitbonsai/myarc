Arc made its first minute feel like an event. Before you opened a single tab, the app had already said hello, and that set the mood for everything after. That's one of the ideas worth keeping, so myArc now has a welcome of its own.

![The myArc welcome, from a dimmed desktop to the browser](welcome.mp4)

*From a dimmed desktop to "myArc, forever yours." in a few seconds.*

## The arrival

For a moment, myArc takes the whole screen. Your desktop dims, and a soft blob in the ribbon's blue, violet, pink and coral wobbles at the centre. It swells past the edges of a window that wasn't there a second ago and becomes it, a midnight field with the same colours drifting slowly behind. The 3D ribbon "A" plays once in the middle, then rises to make room for one line: "myArc, forever yours."

Under that line sits the only real question, which is whether you're bringing anything with you. Import from Arc… turns an Arc export into Spaces, folders and pinned tabs, and Start fresh begins with an empty sidebar. Either way, the window opens up into the browser itself and the sidebar settles in row by row.

The 3D "A" wasn't in the plan. A welcome this big asked for a logo that moves, so it got one, and more polish came along with it. The browser chrome is glassy now, so the sidebar lets the blurred desktop show through, and with Reduce Transparency on it keeps a solid fill. The default colours went darker and warmer, closer to Arc's, and the selected tile in the sidebar got a soft wash in place of a bright grey block.

## A sound of its own

The arrival has its own soundscape, short and ambient, with one accent as the blob becomes the window and another as the headline slides in. It comes out of a small script in the repo that builds it from a fixed seed, so the same notes play every time and anyone can tune them. You only hear it when your Mac's interface sounds are on, and it fades out as soon as you pick a button.

## Learning by doing

After the welcome, a small card points at a real control and asks you to try it. Press ⌘T and open a site. Press ⌘D to pin that tab, so it stays in your sidebar after you close it. Press ⌃2 to hop to another Space, or make your first one if there's only one. Every card has Back and Skip tour, and none of them takes focus, so the shortcut you're practising reaches the browser the same way it will tomorrow.

The last card says "You're ready" and offers to make myArc your default browser if you'd like. The tour waits in Settings, under Advanced, whenever you want to take it again.

## The details

With Reduce Motion on, the dim, the blob and the drifting colours stay out. The welcome fades in with its headline already in place, and the sound stays quiet.

The window keeps a 16:10 shape and stops growing at a comfortable width, so on an ultrawide it sits in the middle like a window instead of stretching into a banner.

If you ever miss the arrival, press ⌃⌥⌘A. It plays again, sound and all, without restarting the tour, and Esc or a click closes it. Consider it a small secret between you and the shortcuts page.

## What's next

A fresh start is getting a little company: a myArc Basics folder with a few short guides, a card that shows where Favorites go, and the command bar already open so you can type straight away.

The new welcome comes with the next nightly on the [download page](https://myarc.dev/download/). If you already use myArc, the replay in Settings is how you'll find it. The code is [on GitHub](https://github.com/bitbonsai/myarc) if you'd like to help make that first minute even better.
